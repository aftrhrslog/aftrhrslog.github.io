title: My memories of exams

Some people say, "Life gets harder after college graduation. Studying as a student is the best part of your life." This saying has made me <mark>reflect</mark> on what my life in school and college was like; looking back, <mark>I can't be certain</mark> that it was the best moment of my life. Honestly, I'm not sure I could be happy if I went back to school.

From high school through college, the one thing I remember is studying for exam <mark>after</mark> exam. I sometimes had <mark>quality time</mark> hanging out with my friends, but only on those days when the exams were finally over. Even worse, <mark>such moments of relief</mark> were <mark>short-lived</mark>, because just a few days after completing my mid-terms or finals, I used to start feeling the pressure again. It was a constant reminder: "You should plan, and start studying for the next exams."

Korean society is overly <mark>fond of</mark> standardized tests as an objective measure of one's qualifications. Achieving good scores is not always easy, but it is the simplest way to secure one's future in the society. Getting <mark>sought-after</mark> jobs in Korea requires passing extensive examinations to earn certificates and other qualifications, and even after being hired, you may need to take an exam <mark>to get promoted</mark>.

I realized this in my teenage years, so in high school, I <mark>adapted quite well</mark> to standardized exams. I knew how to prepare for them, so I used my time and energy efficiently, and usually got the results I wanted. My problem-solving and <mark>test-taking skills</mark> have since helped me <mark>build the career</mark> that I wanted. However, the benefits came with side effects: I <mark>became obsessed with</mark> getting good results, and that obsession has <mark>stayed with</mark> me ever since. I hate the idea of <mark>being ill-prepared</mark>, and I'm too <mark>results-oriented</mark>, unable to enjoy the process. One of my <mark>recurring</mark> nightmares, actually, is about taking an examination. In my dream, I'm a student, sitting in a classroom or a lecture hall, waiting for the <mark>proctor</mark> to hand out exam papers. I can't clearly remember what I need to know to answer the questions, though, because I'm dreaming. So, my <mark>heart starts to beat fast</mark>, and I start to <mark>panic</mark>, <mark>feel extremely anxious</mark>, and worry about <mark>failing the exam</mark>.

It's been nearly a decade since my college graduation, and I thankfully don't need to take any form of exam for the foreseeable future. However, I still often dream about <mark>being unprepared</mark> to take an exam when I'm <mark>under a lot of physical or mental stress</mark>. It's my worst nightmare, in fact, and I'd like to ask other Koreans if they also have recurring nightmares about taking exams.
