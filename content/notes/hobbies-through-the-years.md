My hobbies have changed quite a bit over the years.

In my late twenties, during residency training, I was really into cycling and weight training. Cycling felt like a small weekend adventure. I would pack some water and snacks and sometimes ride 30 kilometers into central Seoul. Weight training gave me a different kind of satisfaction. I enjoyed building my body and especially loved the adrenaline rush after a workout.

In my early thirties, during my military service, English became a serious hobby. I joined a Toastmasters club in Seoul and later became its president. Speaking only English somehow made it easier to connect with people of different ages and backgrounds.

Around the same time, I learned R programming for my research. Coding had been a childhood dream, but not really a hobby. R was mainly a practical tool, and I made small programs that helped with my research and papers.

In my mid-thirties, I got into running, usually 3K or 5K. It was simple but gave me a strong sense of achievement.

Now, in my early forties, intense exercise isn’t as easy on my body, so I prefer brisk walking and light stretching. At the same time, I’ve come back to my old interest in programming through vibe coding.

My hobbies keep changing, but I think I’m always looking for the same things: a clear mind, a little challenge, and the feeling that I’ve accomplished something.
